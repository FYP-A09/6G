Digital Twin Assisted Network Slicing for 6G

SSL, TGNN and MARL inside a Digital Twin. Status as of 22 Oct 2026, including our answers to the four review comments of 12 Oct and what we found when we retrained everything properly.

Sir's four comments

  1. Which datasets do the papers use? Of the 16 papers we reviewed, 8 generate their own data with a simulator, emulator or testbed, only one uses Milan, and none uses a dataset that joins traffic, topology, actions and SLA outcomes.
  2. Is there unified data? No. We checked the 10 datasets we hold and searched for more. The closest (B5G transport traces, single-cell O-RAN traces) have no per-cell allocation actions or SLA outcomes.
  3. So we created it. Real Milan traffic on the real 100 x 100 grid, latency calibrated on the 6G RAN telemetry dataset (SLA-vs-load gap 0.021), load-coupled interference and injected incidents. The Digital Twin dataset has 393.6 million rows and 122 columns: a 400-cell year with 6 what-if scenarios, and all 10,000 Milan cells for the 62 recorded days.
  4. Why MARL and not a single agent? Same reward and training budget, tested on a held-out week. A single centralised agent scores 0.44 / 0.34 / 0.28 at 9 / 25 / 100 cells and gets worse as it grows (its network reaches 604k parameters). The shared-actor MARL scores 0.93 / 0.83 / 0.86 with the same 19k-parameter actor at every size. Trained longer (4,000 episodes, checkpoint chosen on validation days), MARL scores 0.904 at 25 cells and beats a hand-tuned expert rule (0.877) by +0.026 on 7 of 7 test days.

What we are careful about. The expert rule it beats had its two thresholds tuned by grid search on the training days; against an untuned rule the gap is much larger, which proves little. With a short training budget MARL only ties the tuned rule, and when the traffic mix changes (6G growth, massive IoT) without retraining it loses to the tuned rule. The long-training result is at 25 cells; 100 cells has one seed.

What we tell sir today

  • The pipeline runs end to end on real data, and the module results below are all on held-out data against baselines.
  • TGNN forecaster: retrained on 39 days over the whole city, it beats "repeat the last value" by +18% / +33% / +31% (eMBB / URLLC / mMTC) across all 10,000 cells on a held-out week. The version trained on 5 days was worse than that baseline on eMBB, so training length was the problem.
  • Value of the forecast: it lifts the expert slicing rule by +0.03 reward on every one of the 7 test days, about a fifth of the gap to a perfect forecast.
  • SSL: after fixing leaked and duplicated data, raw features reach macro-F1 0.926 on 5G-NIDD. Masked and contrastive pretraining give no reliable gain over raw features at any label budget (50 to all labels). We report this as a negative result.
  • We found and fixed three evaluation problems: split leakage and duplicate flows on NIDD, wrong-slice scoring on B5G, and under-trained learners in our first unified study.

Our approach

  1. Self-supervised learningLearn compact representations from unlabelled telemetry by hiding or corrupting features. Tested against raw features: no gain.
  2. Temporal GNNGraphSAGE over the cell graph, a GRU over time; forecasts the next 10 minutes of demand per slice for every cell.
  3. Multi-agent RLOne shared actor per cell with a neighbourhood critic; each cell chooses its eMBB / URLLC / mMTC split.
  4. Digital TwinA virtual network for training, what-if scenarios and a 393.6 M-row dataset. An approve/reject gate exists only in the early B5G loop.

The idea: slicing that reacts to what the network already did arrives late. A forecast in front of the controller lets it act on what is about to happen. No public dataset has topology, traffic and allocation actions with SLA outcomes together, so we built one from real Milan traffic.

How we check ourselves: every result is on held-out data (a held-out week for the unified work) and next to a simple baseline, including a tuned one.

How we proceed

  1. Strengthen the evidence. More seeds and the long protocol at 9 and 100 cells, a richer tuned expert baseline (per-hour or per-cell targets), and IPPO/MAPPO baselines.
  2. Build the Digital Twin gate so allocations are checked against a one-step look-ahead before they are applied, and measure its effect.
  3. Validate the QoS surrogate against a Simu5G load sweep, the only independent check available.
  4. Write the papers (a review and a forecast-value benchmark) once sir approves the plan.

Present at the final review

  • The unified environment and the 393.6 M-row dataset
  • MARL against a single agent and against a tuned rule
  • The city-wide forecaster and what the forecast is worth
  • The Slice Twin monitor, live
  • Negative results and limitations
